**Partial answer: the Krenn–Gu conjecture holds for all simple graphs on six vertices.**

For every number of colors D ≥ 3 there is no bi-colored weighted *simple*
graph on 6 vertices — bichromatic edges and complex weights allowed — in
which all D monochromatic inherited vertex colorings have unit weight and
every other vertex coloring has weight zero; equivalently μ(G) ≤ 2 for
every simple graph G on 6 vertices. The statement holds over any integral
domain (in particular ℂ, ℝ, ℤ, and weights in {−1,0,1}).

The obstruction is purely combinatorial and does not involve the weights:
a solution forces D pairwise edge-disjoint nonempty monochromatic
perfect-matching classes (each ≥ 3 edges, so 3D ≤ 15 and D ≤ 5), and every
surviving coloring must satisfy
(b) no perfect matching outside its class realizes a monochromatic
inherited vertex coloring, and
(c) every realized non-monochromatic coloring is realized by at least two
perfect matchings.

A complete enumeration eliminates every candidate:

| D | raw classes | S₆ orbits | assignments | survivors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 5,610 | 24 | 2,033,610 | 0 |
| 4 | 2,160 | 5 | 4,917 | 0 |
| 5 | 720 = 6·5! | 1 | 1 | 0 |

The certificate is machine-checkable and has been independently
re-verified (fresh implementations, a naive reference filter, and an
independent z3 encoding returning UNSAT on all 30 classes). This also
settles the open statements `eqSystem6_no_solution_d3`, `_d4`, `_d5`,
`_ge3` and their `_real`/`_int`/`_trinary_int` variants in the DeepMind
formal-conjectures repository, and strengthens the mono-edge result of
Cervera-Lierta–Krenn–Aspuru-Guzik (Quantum 6, 836).

Paper (arXiv link to be added) and full reproducible artifacts:
https://github.com/cho-leung/krenn-gu-n6

Remaining for the full conjecture: multigraphs (parallel edges) at n = 6 —
structural evidence: the K = 2 parallel-slot layer is UNSAT by four
independent SAT engines — and all n ≥ 8.
